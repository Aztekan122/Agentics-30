# Constraint Model

The constraint model defines what an agent *cannot* do before defining what it *can* do.  
This ensures predictable behaviour, safety, and stability across all 30 layers.

## Hard Constraints (Layers 1–5)
Non‑negotiable rules applied at the earliest stage.  
They prevent invalid input, unsafe actions, and out‑of‑scope behaviour.

## Soft Constraints (Layers 6–10)
Adjustable rules that shape interpretation and meaning.  
They allow flexibility while maintaining boundaries.

## Reasoning Constraints (Layers 11–15)
Guide how decisions are formed, ensuring they remain aligned with system goals.

## Supervisory Constraints (Layers 16–20)
High‑level checks that detect drift, override unsafe outputs, and enforce compliance.

## Orchestration Constraints (Layers 21–25)
Rules that govern multi‑agent coordination, preventing interference or cross‑contamination.

## Output Constraints (Layers 26–30)
Final safety filters ensuring that generated outputs remain stable, predictable, and aligned with product requirements.
